# Source policy

## Selection gates

Prefer the closest accountable source for the claim:

1. Official records, specifications, product documentation, source code or original datasets.
2. Peer-reviewed research or a clearly identified preprint when peer review is unavailable.
3. Independent reporting that names its evidence and can be checked.
4. Community material only for experience reports, discovery leads, or clearly labeled opinion.

Do not infer reliability from stars, search rank, professional design, or repeated publication.
Company documentation is primary for its current interface and stated policy, but not independent
evidence for comparative marketing claims. A paper is primary for what it measured, not proof that
its result generalizes beyond its method.

## Retrieval and dates

Open the actual source. Record canonical URL, title, publisher, source type, retrieval time and the
best available publication or update date. For time-sensitive claims, define the answer's as-of time
and reject evidence that cannot establish the relevant period.

Use short excerpts only when they help a reviewer locate the evidence. Preserve the surrounding
context in the locator. Do not copy full articles or paywalled material into artifacts.

## Independence

Assign the same `independent_group` when sources reproduce one press release, dataset, wire story,
paper, or unnamed report. Two URLs from one evidence family count once. Independence is about
provenance, not domains.

## Claim strength

Use the narrowest wording supported by the source. A source reporting an association does not
support causation. Absence of a recorded event does not prove it never happened. A vendor's stated
capability does not prove production performance. Label extrapolation and interpretation as
inference.
